# GR8 Classroom E1 search-intent research

Checked: 2026-08-24

## Method and limits

This time-boxed review used public Google/Bing result patterns, autocomplete-style query expansions visible in results, official UK council term-date sources, the current GR8 catalogue and the available GR8 Search Console facts already recorded for Duck Math. No paid keyword tool or campaign-backed Keyword Planner account was available. Scores are comparative editorial judgements, not search-volume, difficulty, traffic or ranking estimates.

The leading classroom-timer result set was manually reviewed across ten visible results: Kuraplan, Polytimer, ClassroomTimers.app, YouWare, ClassroomRandomizer, SetAlarmClock.net, TimerHub, Lekktura, RepeatingTimer and TicTacTeach. The repeated result pattern was a working browser tool with quick presets, custom duration, large/projector-ready numerals, fullscreen, optional sound and no account. Several recent entrants explicitly advertise background-tab correction, which makes timing accuracy a material quality requirement rather than a decorative feature.

Name-picker results were also tool-led, but require handling pupil names and commonly promote list persistence. UK term-date results were dominated by councils and schools; Croydon and Cornwall both warn that academies, voluntary-aided schools, INSET days or individual schools may differ. That creates a high factual-maintenance burden without a single authoritative national dataset for every school.

## Winner

**Free Classroom Timer / Online Classroom Timer, 88/100.** It has clear recurring utility, global intent, low privacy risk, low factual maintenance, projector and mobile use cases, and a natural opt-in handoff to short games after a session. The result set is competitive but fragmented, and GR8 can add distinct value through accurate elapsed-time handling, calm presentation, explicit privacy, accessibility, and a carefully separated game funnel. The school-calendar alternative does not outweigh it and lacks a safe maintainable source for school-specific dates.

## Search-intent map for the release

- `/classroom/timer`: owns classroom timer, online classroom timer, classroom countdown, countdown timer for classroom and visual classroom timer intent.
- `/classroom`: owns GR8 Classroom navigation, five-minute classroom activities, short classroom games, maths/logic games and calm individual-game discovery without pretending to provide curriculum alignment.
- `/more-free-games/duck-math`: retains the existing canonical. The supplier record describes the game as “Math Duck” while its feed title and stable slug are “Duck Math”; the profile should explain the recognised alternate name rather than creating a competing page.
- No keyword-variant timer URLs, school-calendar database, name picker, group generator or seasonal landing pages are created in E1.

## Evidence observations

- Timer SERPs prefer immediately usable tools over articles. Common expectations: no signup, presets, custom duration, large display, fullscreen and optional sound.
- Recent timer pages explicitly mention timestamp/background-tab correction, providing a concrete quality gap that GR8 must meet.
- Name-picker queries have clear recurring intent, but storing or analysing pupil names raises unnecessary privacy and child-safety risk for the first release.
- Council sources explicitly warn that school-level dates can differ, making a broad calendar database unsuitable without a durable authoritative data pipeline.
- Public maths-game SERPs are crowded by established education/game publishers. GR8 has catalogue fit but should earn relevance through a useful hub and honest selections rather than a broad claim of curriculum coverage.
- Existing Search Console evidence shows the Duck Math URL already receives search interest. That supports contextual inclusion, not a duplicate Math Duck landing page and not a claim that it is the highest-revenue game.
- The supplied 24 July–20 August 2026 Search Console export recorded 19 page clicks and 122 impressions for Duck Math. Those figures are retained as a baseline only; they do not establish demand for the new Classroom routes or prove game-level revenue.

## Evidence limitations

- No defensible exact monthly search volumes, CPCs or keyword-difficulty scores were available.
- Google Trends did not expose a reliable comparable series in the accessible public result surface during the time box.
- People Also Ask and related-search panels vary by location and session; they were treated as qualitative wording evidence only.
- Search Console evidence is limited to the supplied GR8 export facts and is not proof of future demand or rankings.
